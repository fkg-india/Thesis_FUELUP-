# Fuel Up+ Implementation History & Biology Overview

## Part 1: Biological Foundation
Before modeling, it is essential to understand the biological metrics being forecasted.

1. **Glucose (mg/dL or mmol/L)**: The primary sugar found in the blood and the body's main source of energy.
   - **Normal Range**: 70 to 100 mg/dL (fasting).
   - **Hyperglycemia**: >180 mg/dL (Risk of long-term organ damage).
   - **Hypoglycemia**: <70 mg/dL (Risk of immediate fainting/seizures).

2. **Insulin**: A hormone produced by the pancreas that acts as a "key" to let glucose into cells.
   - **Type 1 Diabetes (T1D)**: Pancreas produces zero insulin. External delivery (pump/pen) is required.
   - **Type 2 Diabetes (T2D)**: Cells become resistant to insulin; the body cannot use it effectively.
   - **Forecasting Goal**: Predicting the delay between eating (glucose rise) and insulin action (glucose fall) is the core challenge of Fuel Up+.

## Part 2: Step-by-Step Technical Implementation

### Step 1: Data Unification & EDA
- **Objective**: Standardize 3 diverse datasets (BrisT1D, HUPA, CGMacros).
- **Work Done**: Developed `baselines.py` loaders. Handled unit conversions (mmol/L to mg/dL) and resampled all participant data to 5-minute intervals. Created EDA plots for missingness and diurnal trends.

### Step 2: Baseline Model Implementation
- **Objective**: Establish the "floor" for performance.
- **Work Done**: Implemented Persistence, Ridge Regression, and GBDT (LightGBM). GBDT established a strong benchmark by using macro-nutrient signals (Carbs/Protein/Fat) as static features.

### Step 3: Deep Sequence Modeling
- **Objective**: Use the full 6-hour history (72 steps) to predict the future.
- **Work Done**: Built `deep_models.py` using PyTorch. 
  - **Models**: Gated Recurrent Units (GRU) and Transformer Encoders.
  - **Optimization**: Implemented Apple Metal (MPS) fallback for high-speed local GPU training.
  - **Speedup Strategy**: Shrunk training data to a 10% random subset to allow for 15-minute iteration cycles instead of 5-hour waits.

### Step 4: Uncertainty Quantification
- **Objective**: Quantify "how sure" the model is.
- **Work Done**: Implemented `uncertainty_models.py`.
  - **MC-Dropout**: Sampled the model 30 times at inference to find its variance.
  - **Quantile Regression**: Used Pinball Loss to predict the "Safe Range" (10th/90th percentiles).
  - **Result**: Quantile Regression proved superior for clinical safety (64% coverage vs 22% for dropout).
